# Release names

Every minor release (`0.X.0`) is named after a famous lighthouse: its English common name, taken
in order from the list below (no alphabet rule). Patch releases (`0.X.Y`) inherit the name of their
minor release. Tags stay `vX.Y.Z`; the name appears only in the `CHANGELOG.md` heading
(`## 0.21.0 "Tower of Hercules" (YYYY-MM-DD)`), the GitHub release title and announcements
(`TasmoFleet X.Y.Z "Name"`).

At the release PR, take the next unassigned name and mark it "assigned to X.Y" here. When the list
runs out, add more. Facts below are taken from Wikipedia; a detail that could not be checked is left out.

1. **Tower of Hercules** (assigned to 0.21): A Coruna, Spain. A Roman tower, the only fully preserved Roman lighthouse still used for signalling; UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2009.
2. **Bell Rock** (assigned to 0.22): off Angus, Scotland. First lit in 1811 (Robert Stevenson); the world's oldest working sea-washed lighthouse.
3. **Cape Hatteras** (assigned to 0.23): Buxton, North Carolina, USA. The current tower was first lit in 1871; moved 2,900 ft inland in 1999.
4. **Hook Head** (assigned to 0.24): County Wexford, Ireland. The tower dates from the 12th century and is the second oldest operating lighthouse in the world.
5. **Cape Point** (assigned to 0.25): Cape Peninsula, South Africa. Lit in 1860; the new, lower lighthouse followed in 1919 after the 1911 wreck of the Lusitania.
6. **Skerryvore** (assigned to 0.26): reef southwest of Tiree, Scotland. First lit in 1844 (Alan Stevenson); at 48 m the tallest lighthouse in Scotland.
7. **Cordouan**: mouth of the Gironde, France. Completed in 1611 (Louis de Foix); UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2021.
8. **Cape Byron**: New South Wales, Australia. Lit in 1901 on the easternmost point of the Australian mainland.
9. **Portland Head**: Cape Elizabeth, Maine, USA. First lit in 1791; the first lighthouse built by the US government.
10. **Eddystone**: Eddystone Rocks off Cornwall, England. The first tower was lit in 1698; the current tower, designed by James Douglass, in 1882.
11. **Cape Spear**: near St. John's, Newfoundland, Canada. Lit in 1836; the oldest surviving lighthouse in Newfoundland.
12. **Fastnet**: off County Cork, Ireland. The current tower entered service in 1904; at 54 m the tallest lighthouse in Ireland, nicknamed "Ireland's Teardrop".
13. **Dondra Head**: southern Sri Lanka. Lit in 1890; at 49 m the tallest lighthouse in Sri Lanka.
14. **Split Rock**: Lake Superior, Minnesota, USA. Lit in 1910, built after the 1905 Mataafa Storm.
15. **Bishop Rock**: Isles of Scilly, England. First lit in 1858 and rebuilt in 1887; Guinness lists it as the world's smallest island with a building on it.
16. **Fisgard**: Colwood, British Columbia, Canada. First lit in 1860; the first lighthouse on Canada's west coast.
17. **Kullen**: Kullaberg, Sweden. A lighthouse site since 1563; the current tower was lit around 1900.
18. **Cape Reinga**: Northland, New Zealand. Lit in 1941; the last manned light built in New Zealand.
19. **Boston Light**: Little Brewster Island, Massachusetts, USA. First lit in 1716; the first lighthouse built in what is now the United States.
20. **La Jument**: off Ushant, Brittany, France. Lit in 1911; made famous by Jean Guichard's 1989 photograph of a giant wave.
21. **Longstone**: Farne Islands, England. First lit in 1826; Grace Darling and her father rescued survivors of the Forfarshire from here in 1838.
22. **Cape Otway**: Victoria, Australia. Lit in 1848; the oldest surviving lighthouse on the Australian mainland.
23. **Les Eclaireurs**: Beagle Channel near Ushuaia, Argentina. In service since 1920; often called the "Lighthouse at the End of the World".
24. **Montauk Point**: eastern tip of Long Island, New York, USA. Lit in 1797; approved by Congress in 1792.
25. **Rubjerg Knude**: North Sea coast of Jutland, Denmark. Lit in 1900; moved 70 m inland in 2019 to escape erosion.
26. **Cape Agulhas**: Western Cape, South Africa. Lit in 1849; the second oldest lighthouse still operating in South Africa.
27. **Pigeon Point**: California, USA. Lit in 1872; its 35 m tower ties with Point Arena as the tallest on the US West Coast.
28. **Kannonzaki**: Yokosuka, Japan. Lit in 1869; the first Western-style lighthouse in Japan, built under the French engineer Leonce Verny.
29. **South Stack**: off Holy Island, Anglesey, Wales. First lit in 1809; automated since 1983.
30. **Sambro Island**: entrance of Halifax Harbour, Nova Scotia, Canada. First lit in 1758; the oldest surviving lighthouse in North America.
31. **Cape Bruny**: Bruny Island, Tasmania, Australia. Lit in 1838; Australia's second oldest surviving lighthouse tower.
32. **Roter Sand**: mouth of the Weser, German North Sea. Lit in 1885; the first building erected directly on the open sea floor.
33. **Point Reyes**: Marin County, California, USA. Lit in 1870; officially the windiest and foggiest spot on the Pacific Coast.
34. **Kinnaird Head**: Fraserburgh, Scotland. First lit in 1787 in a tower of an old castle, which now houses the Museum of Scottish Lighthouses.
35. **Jeddah Light**: Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Built in 1990, 131 m tall; a credible claim to the world's tallest lighthouse.
36. **Cape Palliser**: Wellington region, New Zealand. Lit in 1897; 258 steps lead up the cliff to it.
37. **Tillamook Rock**: off Oregon, USA. Lit in 1881 and deactivated in 1957; nicknamed "Terrible Tilly".
38. **Macquarie**: Vaucluse, Sydney, Australia. The first lighthouse in Australia (original tower 1818, Francis Greenway); the current tower dates from 1883.
39. **Peggy's Point**: Peggy's Cove, Nova Scotia, Canada. The current octagonal concrete tower dates from the 1910s and is among the most photographed structures in Atlantic Canada.
40. **Smalls**: rocks west of Pembrokeshire, Wales. The current tower was lit in 1861 (James Douglass); the 1801 story of keeper Thomas Howell led to three-man crews.
41. **Horsburgh**: Pedra Branca, Singapore Strait. Completed in 1851; the island was awarded to Singapore by the International Court of Justice in 2008.
42. **Lindau**: Lake Constance, Germany. First lit in 1856; the only lighthouse in Bavaria.
43. **Cape Wrath**: Sutherland, Scotland. Lit in 1828 (Robert Stevenson) at the most north-westerly point of the British mainland.
44. **St. Augustine**: Anastasia Island, Florida, USA. The current tower was first lit in 1874.
45. **Diamond Head**: Honolulu, Hawaii, USA. First lit in 1899; the current concrete tower dates from 1917.
46. **Cape Florida**: Key Biscayne, Florida, USA. Built in 1825; the Seminole attack of 1836 set the tower on fire.
47. **Hirtshals**: Skagerrak coast, Denmark. First lit in 1863; a 35 m red brick tower.
48. **The Needles**: western tip of the Isle of Wight, England. Built in 1859 on a chalk stack; automated since 1994.
49. **Nobbys Head**: Newcastle, New South Wales, Australia. The headland was lowered from about 43 m to 27.5 m so that ships would not lose wind in their sails.
50. **Muckle Flugga**: north of Unst, Shetland, Scotland. Automated in 1995, when the last residents left.
